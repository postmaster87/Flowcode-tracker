# The FlowCode Mental Game Plan

*Mushin (無心) through a trained routine — Fudoshin (不動心) under fire*

**The system in one line:** the routine is the gate, Fudoshin is the vehicle, "cloud / sky" is the reset. Thoughts and bad results are clouds; your awareness is the sky. You don't fight the cloud — you remember you're the sky.

---

## 1. Your mental bag: two clubs always in hand, one club you choose

Think of every technique as a club in your bag. You'd never hit a single shot trying to use five clubs at once — and the same is true here. **You don't empty the bag onto one shot; you select the one club the moment calls for.**

**Two clubs are always in your hands, on every shot, and you've owned them for years:**

- **The image** — a very small target, ideally just behind your real target, locked from the moment you step behind the ball all the way through the swing.
- **Physiological control** — breath and tension, monitored and corrected with your own methods.

These run constantly underneath everything. They're mastered — but like any club, they stay sharp only with reps, so they stay in your practice rotation even though they no longer need a prompt.

**The third lever is the cue** — the single thing you *add* to crack the door to Mushin. And here's the rule your range session proved in real time: **one cue at a time, never two.** A second cue is swinging two clubs at once — it clutters the mind and kills the shot. The clinic's *"breath + image + cue"* is right as the three lever *types*, and *"one lever rarely works alone"* simply means the cue rides on top of your image-and-breath foundation. It never means stack cues on cues.

So why carry a whole bag if you only use one cue? Two reasons: you pick the *right* cue for the moment, and you keep backups — some days the window opens you up, some days gratitude does. The bag is your options, not your checklist.

## 2. Your pre-shot routine: image + breath + one cue

**Always running (both phases):** your locked image, and your breath-and-tension control. Everything below is built on top of those two constants.

**BUILD-UP — behind the ball.** Image locked, breath settled. Add **one** cue to open toward Mushin. The cues that work for you here — pick a single one:

- a **window** — see the ball fly through an invisible window in the air
- **soft focus** — expanded, relaxed awareness
- **gratefulness**
- **something that makes you happy**

**GAIN — walking in and setting up.** Image still locked, physiology controlled, and your single tempo practice swing (part of your routine for years). Then **one** cue to quiet the mind — pick a single one:

- a **laser** from clubface to your target
- **positive thinking / an affirmation** ("I'm ready and capable")
- **growing a plant** — nurturing it by banking your good shots

**PEAK — the trigger.** Eyes to your locked target, and go on your own tempo. Nothing to add here. The quiet you've built carries the swing. That's Mushin — you only notice afterward how clear it was.

**TRANSITION — after.** Curious, not judgmental. One neutral observation — a fact, never a verdict. *The cloud doesn't change the sky.* Learn one thing, release, walk forward in anticipation.

Bring the **hero-shot attitude to every shot** — full commitment, real energy into the process (*"compete for every shot"*) — but stay **detached from where it lands.** Energy to the process, detachment from the outcome. That's the paradox Fudoshin lets you hold.

## 3. The five nodes you asked me to own

You said train the front of the loop and it drives the back — *"we can train 1–4 and it'll affect 5–8."* That's your leverage. Nail entry, and the swing and the bounce-back take care of themselves.

### Node 1 — FOCUS *("flow follows focus")*

- **The skill:** narrow external focus on one *specific* target. Not "the fairway" — *that* branch, *that* patch of grass.
- **Your tools:** laser, window, target-ball-swing. All visual — your strength.
- **Drill:** before every range ball, name your target out loud and make it as small as you can. The smaller and more specific the target, the quieter the mind. Specificity *is* the off-switch for chatter.

### Node 2 — THOUGHT *(Ken's node)*

- **The skill:** notice the monkey without wrestling it. You can't control the first thought; you own the second. This is the node you work with Ken — his coaching here stands, and this plan rides alongside it, not over it.

### Node 3 — EMOTION *("energy in motion" — Fudoshin)*

- **The skill:** regulate arousal, don't eliminate it. Your note: *"Fear → quickness, tense. Calmness saves."* Tension wants to control the clubface; calm lets it release.
- **Your tools:** gratitude (flips state instantly), the long exhale (down-regulates), *"I am ready and capable"* (confidence).
- **Drill:** rate your arousal 1–10 before each shot. Above a 6, add one extra slow exhale before you step in. You're learning to *feel* your state and adjust it on command.

### Node 4 — STATE — the crucial one *("get into the zone: anchor, visualize, breathwork")*

- **The skill:** drop into the zone reliably via your trained stack. This is the engine. You said it's the crucial node and you're right — it's the gate to Mushin.
- **Your tools:** the full stack in §2.
- **Train it:** make the sequence *fixed and automatic*. Same order, same words, every time, until you're not deciding to do it — it just runs.

### Nodes 7 & 8 — POST-SHOT and RESPONSE

- **Node 7 — post-shot routine:** one neutral observation, release. Every ball, no exceptions.
- **Node 8 — between-shot reset:** step back, soft focus, one breath, then start a *full fresh routine*. Treat each ball as one shot on the course. This single habit transfers more to your scores than any swing change. And remember — no one can touch nodes 1 and 2. Focus and Thought are fully internal and yours, which is why they're your reset levers.

## 4. Driver range protocol

Driver is the right club to train this on: the urge to control and kill it is strongest, so letting go pays the most here.

**Quality over volume. ~25–30 driver balls with the full routine, not 100 rake-and-hits.** Calibration warm-up is informal — don't log it.

**Block 1 — Routine reps (10–12 balls).** Full routine: image locked, physiology controlled, **one** cue per phase, real small target and window. Treat each as a tee shot on a hole you know. Want data? Run a short sub-block on each behind-the-ball cue (window, then soft focus, then gratefulness) and watch your Mushin ratings — that tells you which cue is opening you up *today*.

**Block 2 — Pressure sim (6–8 balls).** Real stakes: fairway or it doesn't count. Miss one and you owe the **Circle Drill**. One cue, hold the discipline, hole the next one.

> **The Circle Drill — your consequence.** 8 putts in a row from 3 feet, set in a full circle around the hole. Every putt must drop. Miss a single one and the whole circle starts over — it is not finished until all 8 are holed consecutively. It's a pressure cooker by design: by putt 7 or 8 the cost of a miss is the entire restart, which is exactly the Fudoshin you're training — stay present, stay unmoved, hole the next one.

**Every ball, both ways:** post-shot routine (Node 7 — one neutral observation, release) and a between-shot reset (Node 8 — step back, soft focus, breathe, re-approach).

**Track the process, not the ball.** Two marks per shot in the tracker: did I run the full routine (Y/N), and Mushin at impact (1–5). Ignore where it landed.

## 5. The arc to scratch (now → August)

Straight with you: 4–5 to scratch in two months is aggressive. But you were near 3 last year, so the ball-striking is *there* — this is about reliably *accessing* it under pressure, which is what the mental game unlocks. *"The mental game is trainable, but it's a skill that must be developed through practice."* Treat scratch as the direction; judge yourself on process, and the number follows.

**Weeks 1–2 — Groove the routine.** Image + breath + one cue, same every time, low pressure. Find your strongest cue for each phase (Block 1's data tells you). *Metric: % of shots with the full routine.*

**Weeks 3–4 — Pressure-proof + transfer.** Make the Circle Drill your standing range consequence. Play practice rounds running the whole loop. Hold the post-shot and between-shot discipline on the course. *Metric: routine holds under on-course pressure; reset quality after bad shots.*

**Weeks 5–6 — Competitive sim.** Rounds that "count" (track score, judge process). Tighten cue selection — fewer, more reliable. *Metric: Mushin average trending up under score pressure.*

**Weeks 7–8 — Tournament application.** Nothing new. Run the trained system in competition; judge only the process goals. *Metric: routine %, reset quality, and the two tracker numbers — the score follows them.*
